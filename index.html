01 · The problem

Why you need a knowledge base

You don't have a note-taking problem — you have a note-finding problem. Everything you save lives in a different app, and none of them talk to each other.

Scattered notes, scattered thinking

  • Twelve places to look. Highlights in one app, PDFs in Drive, screenshots in your camera roll, ideas in a paper notebook.
  • Keyword search only. You remember the idea, not the exact word — so "that note about attention" returns nothing.
  • Write-only folders. You capture 400 articles a year and reread maybe 20. The rest is guilt with a bookmark icon.
  • No connections. The insight you needed in March is unrelated to the one you saved in October — because nothing ever links them.
  • Rewriting the same summary. You've explained "how transformers work" to yourself three times instead of just finding the note.

What AI actually changes

  • Auto-summaries. Every clipped article becomes 5 bullets before you've finished reading the headline. Reading a 6,000-word essay costs you 40 seconds.
  • Suggested tags. The AI reads the content and proposes 3–5 tags from your existing vocabulary, so the taxonomy stays consistent without you policing it.
  • Semantic search. Ask "that paper about spaced repetition and memory" and get it — meaning beats exact keywords every time.
  • Q&A over your notes. "What have I saved about onboarding, and what did I disagree with?" — answered from your vault, with links back to sources.
  • Extracted action items. Meeting notes and long reads get a checklist pulled out automatically, so follow-ups stop disappearing.

Auto-summaries

Long articles, PDFs and meeting transcripts compressed into scannable bullets you'll actually reread.

Smart tagging

Consistent labels applied as you capture, drawn from the tags you already use most.

Semantic search

Search by meaning across your whole vault, not by guessing the title you typed two years ago.

Q&A over notes

Chat with your archive. Every answer cites the note it came from, so you can verify it instantly.

02 · Choose your tools

Notion vs Obsidian

They aren't rivals — they're different layers. Notion is where messy information becomes structured. Obsidian is where structured knowledge becomes yours forever.

Feature-by-feature comparison. “Edge” marks the tool that generally does that job better.
Feature N Notion O Obsidian
Storage Cloud-first. Your pages live on Notion's servers and sync through their app — no local files by default. Plain local Markdown in a folder on your disk (a “vault”). Works fully offline; back it up with Git or any sync tool.Edge
Linking & graph view Mentions and relations exist, but linking is bolted onto a database model. No real graph. [[wikilinks]] are native and fast. The interactive graph shows how ideas cluster and which notes are orphaned.Edge
Databases Best in class: tables, boards, calendars, rollups, relations, formulas, linked views.Edge Not native. The Dataview plugin turns Markdown frontmatter into queryable tables — powerful, but you build it yourself.
Plugins Official integrations and templates; the ecosystem is curated and closed. Limited community extensions. 2,000+ community plugins, plus CSS themes and your own JS. Almost everything is extensible.Edge
Privacy Data sits on their servers; AI features process it in the cloud. Fine for most people, a blocker for some. Files stay on your device. You choose what leaves the machine — ideal for sensitive research or client notes.Edge
Collaboration Real-time multiplayer, comments, sharing and permissions. Built for teams from day one.Edge Solo by default. Publish via Obsidian Publish or share a folder; collaboration means file sync, not live cursors.
Best use case Inbox, tasks, projects, team wikis, meeting notes, content calendars — anything shared or structured. Long-term thinking: reference notes, literature reviews, drafts, journaling, anything you want in 10 years.
The short version

Use Notion as the inbox

Capture is a front-door problem. Notion's web clipper, quick-add buttons and databases make collecting fast and frictionless — exactly what an inbox should be.

The short version

Use Obsidian as the library

Once a note is processed it becomes permanent knowledge. Plain Markdown and local files mean your archive outlives any company, subscription or UI redesign.

04 · Build it

Step-by-step setup

Six steps, roughly a weekend for the first four and an afternoon for the rest. Do them in order — each one makes the next easier.

1

Create the folder structure

Decide where each thing lives before you save anything. A shallow, boring structure beats a clever one you have to think about.

# Obsidian vault 00-Inbox/ # freshly imported, unprocessed 10-Projects/ # active work with a deadline 20-Areas/ # ongoing responsibilities, no end date 30-Resources/ # reference notes by topic 40-Archive/ # done, but searchable Templates/ # note templates live here Home.md # dashboard, linked from the sidebar

2

Set up templates

Every note type gets a skeleton so you never face a blank page. Start with three; add more only when you catch yourself rebuilding the same layout.

  • Article note: source URL, date captured, 5-bullet summary, tags, my take, related notes.
  • Meeting note: attendees, agenda, decisions, action items with owners.
  • Concept note: definition in one sentence, why it matters, examples, counter-arguments, links.
Templater30 minutes
3

Agree a tagging system

Tags are for filtering across the vault; links are for connecting ideas. Keep the tag list small and flat — aim for 15–30 tags total, never nested sub-tags.

  • Topic tags (what it's about): #machine-learning, #city-planning, #nutrition
  • Type tags (what kind of note): #article, #book, #meeting, #idea
  • Status tags (where it is): #inbox, #to-review, #evergreen
Notion select propertyObsidian tags
4

Link notes as you write

Write [[ and pick a note — that's the whole mechanic. Link forward to related ideas and backward to your home dashboard. One note with five links is worth ten orphans.

  • Add a Related section at the bottom of every long note.
  • Run a monthly orphan hunt: search for notes with zero inbound links and either connect or archive them.
  • Use the graph view to spot clusters you didn't know you had.
Graph viewBacklinks
5

Connect an AI tool

Pick one entry point and stick with it for a month. Everything else is optimization.

  • Easiest: turn on Notion AI for summaries, translation and action items directly in your inbox database.
  • For your vault: install the Smart Connections plugin in Obsidian for local semantic search and chat over notes.
  • Power user: point an MCP-capable assistant at a read-only copy of your vault so it can cite real files.
The instruction you give any AI before it touches your notes. 
You are helping me organize my personal knowledge base. Use only the notes I provide as context. When you answer, cite the note title and quote the sentence your answer came from. If the notes don't contain the answer, say so instead of guessing. Suggest tags from this existing list only: article, book, meeting, idea, to-review, evergreen.
Notion AISmart ConnectionsMCP
6

Run a weekly review

Twenty minutes, same day each week. This is the step people skip and the reason systems die. Empty the inbox, accept or reject AI tags, promote keepers to the vault, and archive what you'll never reread.

  • Process: every inbox item becomes “keep” or “delete” — never “look at later”.
  • Connect: add two links or one new tag.
  • Review: read one random old note. It reactivates the memory and often sparks the best ideas.
20 min / weekNon-negotiable
05 · Use it

AI use cases you can copy today

Prompts work in Notion AI, ChatGPT, Claude, or any assistant with access to your vault. The good ones are specific about format and honest about sources.

Summarize on a clipped article

Summarize this article into 5 bullets and suggest tags. Rules: - Each bullet under 25 words - Separate facts from the author's opinion - Suggest 4 tags from my existing tag list - End with one question I should investigate further

Paste article text or the URL
Connect across two topics

Find connections between my notes on spaced repetition and my notes on motivation. For each connection: 1. Quote the exact sentence from each note 2. Explain the link in one line 3. Rate it 1-5 for usefulness 4. Propose a new note title that would sit between them

Attach or paste both sets of notes
Retrieve semantic Q&A

What have I saved about distributed systems consensus algorithms? Answer only from the notes I give you. For every claim, cite the note title and date. If two notes disagree, show both sides instead of picking one.

Answers cite your sources
Extract meeting or call notes

Turn these raw meeting notes into: DECISIONS: what was agreed, verbatim where possible ACTION ITEMS: task, owner, deadline (mark "no owner" if missing) OPEN QUESTIONS: anything unresolved TAGS: 3 tags from my tag list Do not invent owners or deadlines that aren't in the text.

Keeps you honest about gaps
Distill before a talk or essay

I'm writing about [topic]. Here are 12 of my notes on it. Cluster them into 3 themes. For each theme give: - a one-sentence thesis in my voice - the two strongest supporting quotes - the note I should re-read first Then list which notes don't fit anywhere.

Great for drafting
Maintain weekly review

Here are the notes in my inbox this week. For each one say KEEP, MERGE or DELETE with one sentence of reasoning. MERGE means it repeats something I already have - name the existing note. Finish with a 5-line digest of the week.

Run every Sunday
06 · The stack

Recommended tools and plugins

A deliberately short list. Install the free ones first; add paid AI only when you've felt the limit.

Paid add-on Notion

Notion AI

Native assistant that works inside your pages and databases — best for the inbox stage.

  • Summarize long pages and meeting notes
  • Auto-fill properties and suggest tags
  • Extract action items into a task database
  • Ask questions across everything you've shared with it
Free Obsidian plugin

Smart Connections

Semantic search and chat that runs against your local vault — the retrieval layer of this system.

  • Find notes by meaning, not keywords
  • See similar notes while you're writing
  • Chat over a selected folder or tag
  • Runs locally; no data leaves your machine
Free Obsidian plugin

Dataview

Queries your Markdown frontmatter like a database. This is how Obsidian gets close to Notion tables.

  • TABLE and LIST views from tags
  • Auto-generated dashboards per topic
  • "Show all notes missing a summary"
  • Turns your vault into a live index
Free Obsidian plugin

Templater

Scriptable templates — insert today's date, pre-fill tags, and spawn notes with one shortcut.

  • One hotkey creates a fully formatted note
  • Conditional blocks by note type
  • Keeps your tagging rules enforced
  • Pairs with the folder structure in step 1
Free Browser extension

Web Clipper

The official Notion Web Clipper (or Obsidian's own) is the front door of the whole system.

  • Saves page, title and URL in one click
  • Pre-selects your inbox database
  • Remove clutter before it reaches AI
  • Set a default tag like #inbox for all clips
Advanced Integration

MCP & API integrations

For when you want one assistant to read Notion and your Obsidian vault at the same time.

  • Notion API: read/write pages and databases
  • Obsidian Local REST API plugin: expose the vault
  • MCP servers glue both into a single chat
  • Keep it read-only until you trust it
07 · Stay out of trouble

Tips and common mistakes

Most knowledge bases fail from too much system, not too little. These are the four habits that keep yours alive.

Habits that work

Keep it simple

Two apps, three templates, one inbox, one folder tree. If explaining your system takes longer than 60 seconds, it's too complex.

Avoid over-tagging

Five precise tags beat twenty vague ones. If a tag has fewer than three notes using it, merge it — single-use tags are just filenames in disguise.

Review regularly

Twenty minutes a week beats three hours a quarter. Reviews are where capture turns into knowledge — everything before that is just storage.

Write in your own words

Let AI summarize, but add one line of "what this means for me". Notes without your opinion are just a second bookmark folder.

Keep a plain-file exit

Markdown in a folder means you can always leave. Back up the vault weekly — sync is not a backup.

Mistakes to avoid

Building the cathedral first

Three weeks of folder design and zero saved notes. Start messy with the default structure and refine after note #50.

A tag for every idea

#article-about-ml is not a tag, it's a title. Tags classify; titles describe. Keep them from fighting over the same job.

Trusting AI citations blindly

Models paraphrase confidently. Always click through to the source note before you quote your own vault to someone else.

Two inboxes, no owner

If Notion and Obsidian both accept raw captures, you'll check both forever. Notion captures; Obsidian stores. Never the reverse.

Skipping the weekly review

The inbox becomes a graveyard within a month. Calendar it, keep it to 20 minutes, and never move it to "someday".

Adding AI tools before a habit

Prompt engineering won't save an unused vault. Get capture and review running for two weeks, then add intelligence.

08 · Answers

Frequently asked questions

The five things people ask before starting.

What does this actually cost?

The core system is free: Obsidian is free for personal use, Notion's free plan is plenty for a single user, and every plugin recommended here is free. Paid extras are optional — Notion AI is a per-seat add-on, and Obsidian Sync/Publish cost money only if you want their official sync instead of a free alternative like iCloud, Dropbox or Git. A student can run the whole thing for $0.

Is my data private? Who can read my notes?

Obsidian stores plain Markdown files on your device — nothing leaves it unless you add a sync or publishing service. Notion pages live on Notion's servers. The key rule: decide what kind of note goes where. Keep medical, financial, client or unpublished research material in Obsidian; keep shared projects and public reference material in Notion. Also read the current AI settings for any tool before you paste sensitive text into it.

Do Notion and Obsidian sync with each other?

Not automatically — and that's fine. Treat it as a one-way pipeline, not a mirror. Notes graduate from the Notion inbox to the Obsidian vault during your weekly review: export or copy the processed note, drop it into 00-Inbox, and let Obsidian's importer convert it to Markdown. If you want automation later, the Notion API can export pages on a schedule, but a manual weekly handoff is more reliable and keeps you reviewing your notes.

I'm a complete beginner — is this too much?

Start with step 1 and step 6 only: create the folders, then spend 20 minutes a week moving notes. Skip Dataview, Templater and MCP entirely for the first month. Once capture and review feel automatic, add one AI feature — most people start with summaries, because it's visible value with no configuration. Everything else in this guide is an upgrade you add later, not a prerequisite.

Which one should I start with?

Start with Notion if you already capture a lot and organize nothing — its inbox is forgiving. Start with Obsidian if you already have folders of Markdown, or if local files and privacy matter more to you. If you're truly new, use Notion alone for two weeks to build the capture habit, then set up the Obsidian vault when you notice your inbox getting heavy.
